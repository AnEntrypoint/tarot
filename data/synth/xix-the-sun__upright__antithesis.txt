In the radiant light of XIX The Sun, the dialectical dance of opposing forces is set ablaze. The warmth of material happiness and fortunate marriage, as Waite so aptly described, is tempered by the clarifying fire of the Sun, which casts a harsh light on the contradictions of human experience. The brightening of understanding, as Etteilla's Eclaircissement suggests, reveals the shadowy undertones of desire and the development of the self, while the elemental force of fire, as the Golden Dawn's Hebrew Resh and Crowley's elemental associations attest, burns away the comforting illusions of complacency, leaving only the stark clarity of the present moment.
