In the Two of Cups, the dialectic of desire and its resolution is set in stark relief. The card's traditional associations with love, passion, and friendship belie a more complex, even contradictory, nature: it is also a card of union, concord, and sympathy, suggesting a harmony that is not merely a given, but a hard-won resolution of opposing forces. The card's favourable aspects - its promise of wealth and honour - only serve to underscore the tension between the individual's desires and the external world, a tension that can only be resolved through a dialectical process of negotiation and compromise.
