In the Two of Wands, the dichotomy of desire and disillusionment is distilled into a singular, resolute figure, the lord of his dominion, who gazes out upon a world of contrasts. Riches and suffering, magnificence and mortification, are balanced on the scales of his contemplation, as if the very fabric of reality were a ledger to be weighed and measured. The tension between the two wands, the staff of power and the staff of suffering, dissolves into a higher unity, a synthesis that transcends the opposition, a re-framing that reconfigures the terms of the debate.
