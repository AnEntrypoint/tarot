The Wheel of Fortune, a card of destiny and elevation, turns inexorably, its cyclical motion a manifestation of the dialectical process that governs all of life. As Waite's description suggests, it is a card of success and felicity, where the individual's fortunes are elevated and their prospects improved, a blessing that brings wealth and prosperity. Yet, as Etteilla's Augmentation notes, this is not a guarantee, for fortune is a double-edged sword, bringing both luck and misfortune, happiness and despair, in its relentless turn.
