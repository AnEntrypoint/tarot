In the inverted stance of the Hanged Man, the figure's limbs, once outstretched in the pursuit of progress, now constrict, as if bound by the very forces that once propelled them. The crowd, that great, faceless body politic, has ensnared the individual, rendering them immobile and mute, their voice lost in the din of the nation's generational din. The element of Water, that fluid, life-giving force, now congeals, its flow blocked by the weight of the self, selfishness, and the crowd's suffocating pressure.
