In the Two of Wands, the tension between opposing forces is palpable, a dynamic that has been turned inward, its usual expansive and adventurous spirit now stifled, its energies constricted and uncertain. The usual sense of mastery and control, of dominance and authority, is replaced by a feeling of being stuck, of being trapped in a cycle of fear and confusion, where the usual certainties of the past are no longer reliable, and the future is shrouded in uncertainty.
