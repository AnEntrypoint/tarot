In the Six of Wands, the triumphant figure, now reversed, stands at the gate of a desolate landscape, the gates of victory having been thrown open to the enemy within. The fire that once fueled their conquest now flickers in the darkness, a dying ember of confidence, as the weight of treachery and disloyalty crushes their resolve. The deferred hope of triumph is reduced to a hollow expectation, a nagging fear that the enemy at the gate is not without, but within.
