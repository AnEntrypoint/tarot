In the Nine of Cups, the antithesis of the traditional notion of happiness, we find a figure beset by the contradictions of its own sincerity. The card's usual associations with truth, loyalty, and liberty are here inverted, its boldness and ingenuousness stifled, its familiarity and intimacy turned inward, as if the very qualities that once brought joy are now sources of pain and regret. The card's usual optimism, a product of its elemental connection to water, is here drained, leaving only a hollow, unfulfilled longing.
