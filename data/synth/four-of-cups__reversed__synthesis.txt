In the Four of Cups, reversed, we find a force typically associated with new knowledge, new directions, and new information, now stymied, its flow blocked or turned inward. The usual promise of novelty, a harbinger of new understanding, has become a mere echo, a faint glimmer of what once was, now stifled by the weight of its own expectations. The luxury of new possibilities, once a guiding light, now feels oppressive, a burden of its own making.
