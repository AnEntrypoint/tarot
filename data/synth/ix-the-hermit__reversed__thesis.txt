In the inverted position of the Hermit, the figure of the solitary seeker of truth is revealed in a state of arrested development, his introspective gaze turned inward, his eyes cast downward, as if the weight of his own concealment has become too much to bear. His normally steady hand now falters, his staff lies still, and his usually steady footsteps falter, as the hermit's own fear of being found out, of being unmasked, of being unmasked, has become a crippling self-doubt.
