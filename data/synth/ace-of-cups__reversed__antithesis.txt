In the Ace of Cups, the fluid, receptive, and nurturing force of the suit is turned against itself, its usual flow stymied and redirected inward, as if the very source of emotional sustenance has become a source of turmoil. This is not a gentle, soothing influence, but a turbulent and often painful one, as the normally expansive and generous qualities of the suit are now constricted and turned against the self. The usual certainties and expectations of the suit are upended, leaving the querent feeling unmoored and adrift.
