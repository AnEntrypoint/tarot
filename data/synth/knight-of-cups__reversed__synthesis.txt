In the Knight of Cups, reversed, we find a figure whose artful nature is turned inward, his subtlety and ruse now directed against himself. His fire, once a beacon of inspiration, is reduced to a smoldering ember, a feeble flame that casts flickering shadows on the walls of his own psyche. This is not a reversal of his essential nature, but rather a distortion, a corruption of the fluid, adaptable spirit that once drove him to navigate the complexities of the world with ease and guile.
