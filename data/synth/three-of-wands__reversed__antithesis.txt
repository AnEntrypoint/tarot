In the Three of Wands, the spark of creation and progress is stifled, its flames reduced to smoldering embers. The dynamism of the suit of Wands, with its associations of action and initiative, is now turned inward, its energies spent on self-preservation rather than expansion. The tension between the desire for progress and the reality of stagnation creates a sense of frustration and impotence, a feeling of being trapped in a cycle of toil and disappointment.
