In the Four of Wands, the tranquil surface of country life, a haven of refuge, is disrupted by the stirrings of discordant forces, the unquiet energies that threaten to upend the perfected work of concord and prosperity. The associations of assembly, connection, and federation, once harmonious, now begin to fray, as the element of fire, in its third decan, ignites a spark of tension within the established order, hinting at the possibility of unexpected good fortune, a turn of events that will shatter the complacent calm and propel the individual into a new, uncharted terrain.
