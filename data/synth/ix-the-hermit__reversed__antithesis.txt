In the inverted stance of the Hermit, the figure of the anchorite, once a beacon of solitude and introspection, now cowers in the shadows, its light extinguished by the weight of its own concealment. The earthy, grounded essence of Virgo, typically a source of stability and practicality, is here warped and distorted, its usual virtues turned against themselves, fueling a self-imposed exile from the world. The hermit's fear of being seen, of being known, has become a self-perpetuating cycle of unreasoned caution, a policy of concealment that has turned inward, suffocating the very soul that once sought refuge in its solitude.
