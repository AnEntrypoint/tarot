The figure of the Ten of Wands approaches with a burden, weighed down by the rods that beset him, their oppressive presence a palpable force that threatens to stifle all progress. Success, gain, and fortune are within his grasp, yet they are also the source of his suffering, a double-edged sword that cuts both ways, promising and oppressing. In this paradoxical state, the lines between good and bad, right and wrong, are blurred, and the figure is beset on all sides by the contradictions that threaten to consume him.
