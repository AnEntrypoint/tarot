The Two of Swords embodies a precarious balance, a harmony that is both fragile and fleeting. Waite's observations of its potential for conformity and concord, as well as Etteilla's emphasis on friendship and tenderness, suggest a card that is both a refuge and a constraint. Yet, as Waite notes, this harmony is not without its qualifications, and the card's true nature is one of tension and resolution, a synthesis that dissolves the oppositions, a higher unity that absorbs the discord.
