In the Seven of Wands, a figure stands resolute, its enemies arrayed against it, yet it holds its ground, unyielding and unyielding. On the plane of intellect, this is a card of contentious debate, of verbal sparring and haggling, where each side seeks to outmaneuver the other in a battle of wits and wills. The very position of the figure, atop the hill, bespeaks its unshakeable resolve, a stance that brooks no compromise, no quarter given or taken.
