In the stark, unadulterated light of the Fool, we behold the abyss of unreason, the precipice of unbridled enthusiasm, where the rational faculties are reduced to a mere fluttering of the senses. Waite's "folly" and Etteilla's "insanity" converge in a vortex of unrelenting fervor, an intoxication that dissolves the boundaries of prudence and decorum, leaving the individual lost in a sea of unreason. Against this backdrop, the rational, the grounded, the measured, and the wise must stand as the antithesis, the opposing force that seeks to anchor the unmoored and the ungrounded.
