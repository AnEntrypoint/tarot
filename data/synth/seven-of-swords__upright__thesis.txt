The Seven of Swords presents a design that is uncertain in its import, as the disparate significations of hope, confidence, and futility converge in a state of tension. Waite's observations of the card's promise of a country life after a competence has been secured, juxtaposed with Etteilla's emphasis on hope, await, and expectation, suggest a dichotomy between the actuality of a secure future and the fantasy of one yet to be realized.
