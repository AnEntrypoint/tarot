In the inverted stance of the Hermit, the figure of the solitary seeker of truth is revealed as a fragile, self-enclosed entity, its usual reserve and discernment now a brittle, defensive shield. The hermit's withdrawal from the world, once a noble pursuit of wisdom, has become a fearful, unreasoned caution, a concealment of the self from the world, and the world from the self. This is not the stillness of contemplation, but the stasis of anxiety, the hermit's inner world a cramped, claustrophobic space where the seeker is lost to its own fears and doubts.
