The Six of Swords, in its upright position, is a declaration of a clear and resolute will, a public announcement of one's intentions, a permit to proceed with confidence. But in its reversed state, this same force is turned inward, blocked, and weakened, becoming a confession of defeat, a denunciation of one's own desires, a revelation of the futility of one's efforts. The element of Air, which once carried the promise of intellectual freedom, now suffocates under the weight of its own limitations, a permit revoked, a license to pursue revoked.
