In the Eight of Pentacles, a once-disciplined and ingenious mind is now mired in the quagmire of its own cupidity, its cunning and intrigue turned against itself. The possession of skill has curdled into usury, an exorbitant and extortionate grasping for advantage, avarice that has become a self-perpetuating cycle of privation. The once-prudent and practical individual is now compromised, their prudence turned inward, their earthy virtues lost in the quagmire of their own making.
