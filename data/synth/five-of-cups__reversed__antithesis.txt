In the Five of Cups, the figure stands at the precipice of a profound disconnection, their gaze cast backward in a futile attempt to recapture the past, as if the very fabric of their relationships has unraveled. The reversed position of the figure, a stark contrast to the upright stance of the upright card, underscores the crippling sense of loss and disaffection that pervades this reading, a disconnection from the blood ties that once bound them to their kin, their ancestors, and their very sense of self.
