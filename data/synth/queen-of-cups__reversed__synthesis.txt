In the Queen of Cups, a figure of refined sensuality, we find a synthesis of opposing forces: the desire for emotional fulfillment and the reality of corruption. Waite's accounts of her as a "distinguished woman" and "perverse woman" suggest a complex, multifaceted nature, while Etteilla's descriptions of "dishonesty" and "debauchery" imply a darker, more troubled aspect. Reversed, this card's energies are turned inward, its waters stagnant and blocked, a reflection of the self-destructive tendencies that can arise when the pursuit of pleasure becomes a means of self-undoing.
