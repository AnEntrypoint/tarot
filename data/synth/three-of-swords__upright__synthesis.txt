In the Three of Swords, the fragmentation of the self is starkly rendered, a dispersal of the psyche into the shards of its own making. Waite's catalog of removals and absences - flight, departure, dispersion - bespeaks a disintegration of the self, a dissolution of the boundaries that once held it fast. Yet, this is not a card of despair, but rather the crucible in which the self is reassembled, refigured, and reconstituted in a new, higher unity, one that absorbs the contradictions and antagonisms of its constituent parts.
