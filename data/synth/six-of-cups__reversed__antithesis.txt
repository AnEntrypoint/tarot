In the Six of Cups, reversed, the fluid, dreamy promise of the future is stymied, its gentle lapping at the shore of reality blocked by the weight of what's come before. The inheritance of memories, the accumulation of experience, the sediment of past joys and sorrows, all congeal into a heavy, unyielding mass that resists the forward flow of time, the tide of what's to come. The pleasure of the present moment, that fleeting, shimmering thing, is lost in the undertow of what's been, the weight of what's been left behind.
