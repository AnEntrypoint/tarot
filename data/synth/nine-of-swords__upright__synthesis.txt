In the Nine of Swords, the figure of the ecclesiastic, a symbol of spiritual authority, is beset on all sides by the weight of despair, a crushing burden that threatens to consume them whole. This is the card of disappointment and failure, where the aspirant to spiritual authority is instead confronted with the abyss of their own doubt and uncertainty. The tension between the desire for transcendence and the reality of limitation is here resolved not in a compromise, but in a re-framing that acknowledges the impossibility of the ideal, and the necessity of the real.
