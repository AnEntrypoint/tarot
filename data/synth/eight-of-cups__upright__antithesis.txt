The Eight of Cups, a card of seeming contradictions, presents a paradoxical figure: a woman of reserve and timidity, yet possessing a certain allure, a quiet strength that belies her modesty. Waite notes that, in practice, the card often signifies the decline of a matter, a diminishment of importance, while Etteilla's description of the card as a "blond girl" and "honest girl" suggests a certain moral rectitude, a sense of honor and discretion. Crowley's association with Saturn in Pisces, a sign of limitation and restriction, underscores the card's themes of restraint and opposition, highlighting the tension between the desire for freedom and the need for discipline.
