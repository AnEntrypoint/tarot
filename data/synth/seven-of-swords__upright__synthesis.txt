The Seven of Swords embodies a tension between disparate significations, its design uncertain in its import due to the conflicting nature of its attributes. Waite's observations of design, attempt, wish, and hope, on the one hand, are at odds with his notes on quarrelling, a plan that may fail, and annoyance, on the other. This dichotomy is reflected in the card's promise of a country life after competence has been secured, a prospect that is both tantalizing and precarious, as if the very notion of stability is itself a fragile thing, subject to the whims of the querent's will.
