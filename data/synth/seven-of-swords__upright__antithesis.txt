The Seven of Swords embodies the tension between the desire for independence and the fear of failure, a dichotomy that underscores the uncertainty of its design. Waite's conflicting significations - design, attempt, wish, hope, confidence - reflect the card's ambivalence, a sense of quarrelling between opposing forces. Etteilla's emphasis on longing and expectation, however, suggests a more optimistic tone, one that anticipates a country life after a competence has been secured, a promise that is undercut by the card's association with futility, as seen in Crowley's title, "Futility".
