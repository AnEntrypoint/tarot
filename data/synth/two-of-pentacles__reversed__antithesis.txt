In the Two of Pentacles, reversed, we find the stifled potential of creation, a composition blocked from flowing freely. The enforced gaiety of the upright position gives way to a stifling sense of injustice, a simulated enjoyment that's been reduced to a hollow mockery of its former self. The elements of production, once so neatly arranged, now writhe in a cramped and twisted manner, as if the very principles of creation itself have been turned against the self, rendering the written work a mere letter of exchange, a bill for a debt that can never be paid.
