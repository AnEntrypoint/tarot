In the Two of Pentacles, the practical and material world is beset by enforced gaiety, a stifling of genuine enjoyment that arises from the stifling of creative potential. The simulated enjoyment that remains is a paltry substitute for the real thing, a paltry commerce of letters and bills that only serves to underscore the ignorance of those who would seek to profit from it. The element of Earth, typically associated with stability and structure, is here turned against itself, its principles of composition and production twisted into a self-perpetuating cycle of debt and obligation.
