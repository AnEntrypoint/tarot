In the Seven of Wands, a figure stands poised on the precipice of indecision, weighed down by the burdens of his own making. The wands, once tools of creation and action, now lie broken and still, a testament to the crippling anxiety that has rendered him immobile. His valour, that fiery spark of the element, is smothered by the weight of his own doubts, and he stands as a monument to the perils of irresoluteness, a cautionary tale of what happens when the will to act is consumed by the whims of the mind.
