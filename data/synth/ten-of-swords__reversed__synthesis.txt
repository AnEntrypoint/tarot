In the Ten of Swords, the force of success and advantage is turned inward, its usual trajectory blocked or weakened, rendering it ineffective, its power and authority spent. The usual benefits of victory and fortune, the spoils of war, are now nothing more than a hollow echo, a faint memory of what once was, a mere shadow of what once was possible. The element of Air, associated with reason and intellect, is now mired in the muck of its own limitations, its clarity and precision lost to the murk of its own self-doubt.
