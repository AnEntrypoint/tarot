In the Eight of Swords, the weight of opposition and adversity is palpable, yet its reversal suggests not a reprieve, but a reorientation, a rechanneling of the same forces that once constricted. The Eight, once a symbol of confinement and limitation, now appears as a vortex of internalized tension, a self-imposed prison of one's own making, where the lines of force and resistance are inextricably entwined.
