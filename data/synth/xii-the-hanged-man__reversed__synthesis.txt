In the inverted stance of the Hanged Man, the individual's will is suspended, their selfish desires and attachments severed from the crowd, the body politic, and the generational tides that shape the nation. This is not a moment of stasis, but rather a moment of re-framing, where the dialectical forces of the self, the people, and the sovereign are momentarily still, awaiting the resolution that will reabsorb the tension. The Hanged Man's reversal is a moment of privation, a turning inward of the will, where the element of Water, the fluid and receptive force, is blocked, and the individual is forced to confront the limits of their own desires.
