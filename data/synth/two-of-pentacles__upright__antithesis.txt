In the Two of Pentacles, the dialectical tension between gaiety and trouble, between recreation and obstruction, is palpable. The card's design, a tableau of coins, suggests a marketplace of exchange, where value is constantly being weighed and re-weighed, but also where obstacles and embarrasment are ever-present, like the cloudy, muddled atmosphere that pervades the scene. Waite's observation that troubles are more imaginary than real only underscores the card's paradoxical nature, where the very act of weighing and calculating can itself become a source of trouble, a predicament that the card's design seems to embody.
