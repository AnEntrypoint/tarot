The Eight of Cups, a card of seeming contradictions, presents itself as a paragon of virtue, yet in practice, it often signifies the decline of a matter, its importance diminished by the passage of time or the gaze of hindsight. This reserved and modest figure, a blond girl of honor and discretion, embodies the tension between the ideal and the actual, her sweetness and timidity masking a deeper sense of uncertainty and apprehension. In its synthesis, the Eight of Cups dissolves this tension, resolving the oppositions that define it, its true nature revealed only in the quiet, unassuming way of the still waters that flow beneath its surface.
