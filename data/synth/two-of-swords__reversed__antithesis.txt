In the Two of Swords, the antithesis of harmony and balance, we find the dissonant undertones of duplicity and disloyalty, a duplicity that arises from the very fabric of the self, a self that is rent asunder by the contradictions of its own making. The once-steady scales of justice are now askew, the weighing of reason and emotion, now a seesaw of deceit and bad faith, the calculated dissimulation of a heart turned inward, a heart that is its own worst enemy.
