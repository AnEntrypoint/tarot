In the Three of Swords, the figure of the woman stands alone, her back turned to the viewer, her face shrouded in sorrow, her presence a negation of the warmth and intimacy that typically accompanies the heart. The absence of her lover, the distance that separates them, is a palpable thing, a weight that presses upon her, a source of pain and longing that cannot be assuaged. Her isolation is a state of being, a condition of the soul, a negation of the very idea of love and connection.
