In the Six of Swords, the force of declaration and confession, once a liberating and expansive impulse, is now turned inward, its energy stifled by the constraints of the material world. The once-clear voice, now muffled, is reduced to a mere whisper, a private and self-referential murmur that fails to break free from the confines of the self. This is not a compromise, but a re-framing, a reorientation of the original impulse, one that seeks to reabsorb the contradictions and paradoxes that once drove it forward.
