In the Four of Pentacles, the tension between the desire for material security and the uncertainty of the future is palpable. The card's emphasis on possessions and inheritance, as Waite notes, is a double-edged sword, for while it offers a sense of surety, it also risks ossifying one's being, making it resistant to change and innovation. This is particularly evident in the card's reversed position, where the focus on possessions becomes an all-consuming anxiety, a grasping that cannot be satiated.
