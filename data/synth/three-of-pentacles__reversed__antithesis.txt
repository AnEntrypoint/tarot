In the Three of Pentacles, we find the mediocrity of the material world, its puerility and pettiness laid bare. The usual trappings of success and accomplishment are here reduced to a mere trifling, a childish fixation on the mundane and the trivial. The earthy, practical nature of the suit is inverted, its usual solidity and dependability turned into a brittle, fragile thing, its strength sapped by the petty concerns and weaknesses that beset it.
