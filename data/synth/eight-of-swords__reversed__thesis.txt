The Eight of Swords, in its reversed position, bespeaks a situation where the individual's agency is crippled by their own self-doubt and indecision, their capacity for action stymied by the weight of their own indecisiveness. This is not a state of freedom from the constraints of external circumstance, but rather a self-imposed prison of mental and emotional paralysis, where the individual's own fears and anxieties have become the primary obstacles to their progress.
