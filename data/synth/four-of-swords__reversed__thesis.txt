The Four of Swords, in its upright position, bespeaks a state of wise administration, where economy, good conduct, and foresight govern the affairs of the individual. A qualified success may be expected from such a prudent approach, one that balances caution with discernment. In its reversed position, however, this same force is turned inward, its energies blocked or diverted, leading to a state of avarice, where the very qualities of wise administration are perverted, and instead of prudent management, there is a self-interested, calculating approach that prioritizes gain over all else.
