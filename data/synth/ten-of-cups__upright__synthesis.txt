In the Ten of Cups, the querent finds a state of contentment that is both a repose of the entire heart and the perfection of human love and friendship. This is not a static state, but a dynamic synthesis, a higher unity that absorbs the tensions of desire and fulfillment, of expectation and reality. The querent's interests are taken care of, their needs met, and they are at home in their own skin, in their own village, their own country, their own abode.
