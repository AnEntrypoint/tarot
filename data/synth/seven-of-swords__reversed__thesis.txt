In the Seven of Swords, the figure stands at the precipice of futility, their feet planted firmly on the precipice of reason, yet their counsel, once wise and well-intentioned, now falters and falls short of its mark, its words reduced to a mere babble of self-justification. The once-clear voice of reason, now muffled and uncertain, struggles to find purchase in a world that no longer listens, its warnings and announcements lost in the din of a world that has grown weary of its own advice.
