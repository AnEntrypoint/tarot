In the Three of Cups, the usual narrative of joy and fulfillment is disrupted, its energies turned inward, its abundance now a privation. The expedition of pleasure and satisfaction is replaced by a sense of blockage, a dispatch stalled, a business transaction gone awry. The element of Water, associated with emotions and the subconscious, is now constricted, its flow impeded, as the usual consolations of life are withdrawn, leaving only a hollow sense of termination.
