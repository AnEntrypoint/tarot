In the stillness of the Hermit, the contradictions of the world are resolved, the dialectical oppositions of prudence and deception, of truth and falsehood, of the virtuous and the treacherous, are sublated, transcended, and reabsorbed into a higher unity. The Hermit's isolation is not a withdrawal from the world, but a withdrawal into the world, a withdrawal into the very heart of things, where the distinctions of the external world are seen to be but a momentary flux, a play of forces that are ultimately one.
