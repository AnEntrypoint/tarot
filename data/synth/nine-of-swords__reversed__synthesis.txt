In the Nine of Swords, we find the crystallization of a force that has been turned inward, its sharp edges blunted by the weight of its own self-doubt. This is not a card of mere suspicion, but of justifiable mistrust, a prudent wariness born of experience and a healthy dose of shame. The element of Air, associated with reason and intellect, is here clouded by the dark undertow of fear, as the mind is beset by the cruel whims of its own imagination.
