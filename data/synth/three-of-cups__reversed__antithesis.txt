In the Three of Cups, the usual effusions of joy and contentment are inverted, as if the very abundance that once overflowed has turned to bitter excess. The pleasure-seeking impulse, once a liberating force, now festers, a self-indulgent indulgence that has lost all sense of proportion, leaving only a hollow, aching void. The dispatch of desires, the execution of pleasure, has become a one-way street, a dead end.
